# V3.5 monetisation research and decisions

> **V3.5.1 product-direction note:** This research remains the evidence record for V3.5 monetisation, but the board-frame cosmetic implementation chosen in V3.5 was later rejected. V3.5.1 archives that collection and reserves Cosmetic Credits for future full themes. See [V3.5.1-ACCOUNT-COSMETIC-PATCH.md](V3.5.1-ACCOUNT-COSMETIC-PATCH.md) for the current product decision.

Research date: 6 October 2026. Baseline: V3.4 `816c6f23dd67e25b3590610c0d515538a0ce6c38`.

## Executive decision

Use a hybrid of voluntary purchases, optional rewarded ads, and a tightly limited automatic-ad pilot. Sell personal expression, convenience and a quieter experience, not wins. Do not make the free game worse to manufacture purchases. All numerical prices, caps and experiment thresholds below are authored starting hypotheses, not industry optima or revenue forecasts.

The research reviewed original academic work, first-party implementation documentation, publisher case studies and regulator statements. The first discovery pass returned 26 candidates across purchase psychology, publisher ad experience, ad policies and currency transparency; duplicate papers and secondary mirrors were excluded. Sources are listed below so evidence can be distinguished from our design choices.

## 1. What actually motivates purchases?

Hamari et al. (2017) surveyed 519 purchasers and grouped 19 concrete motivations into six dimensions. Unobstructed play, social interaction and economic rationale were positively associated with spending. Competition and unlocking content were not universal predictors. This is self-reported, correlational evidence, not proof that adding an obstacle causes profitable purchases [R1]. A second survey across 2,791 respondents found a more complicated relationship between enjoyment, continued play and purchase attitudes [R2]. Neither paper establishes a conversion rate for Mega XO.

The practical implication is to give a player something worth wanting after they enjoy the game. For Mega XO: a visible frame or board surround that expresses identity; a reasonably sized Crown pack with its actual purchasing power explained; an inexpensive one-time introduction to cosmetics; and permanent removal of automatic interruptions. Competitive strength, opponent selection, Elo, timers, moves and rank requalification remain unavailable for purchase.

A qualitative grounded-theory study distinguishes functional, ornamental and probability-based goods. It describes purchases prompted by discomfort, exposure to cosmetics and restricted access [R3]. Its explanations are hypotheses from qualitative evidence, not an instruction to induce anxiety. Mega XO will use accurate cosmetic previews and clear benefits, not deliberately frustrating matchmaking or a loss-triggered offer.

### Subtle persuasion versus deceptive design

Several mechanisms can influence a decision without a player consciously analysing each one:

- **Reference prices and anchoring:** a larger pack creates a comparison point. Use an accurate unit-price comparison where localized prices are available. Do not invent a crossed-out price, a fake saving or a decoy whose only purpose is to make another pack look inexpensive.
- **Mental accounting:** virtual currencies separate acquisition from later spending. Explain 10 Coins = 1 Crown and show real store prices; do not add conversion steps or conceal leftover currency.
- **Commitment and ownership:** a preview can help a player imagine their own board. Preview and purchase must be distinct actions. Equipping owned cosmetics is free.
- **Identity and social expression:** appearance has value without affecting match outcomes. Cosmetics must actually be visible, not merely an inventory label.
- **Convenience:** removing interruptions is understandable. Do not increase ads after someone declines to pay or loses a game.
- **Reciprocity/support:** players may wish to support a game they enjoy. This can inform purchase messaging, but Google's rewarded-ad policy specifically disallows messaging such as watching an ad to support the business. Rewarded CTAs describe only the ad action and promised reward [R5].
- **Scarcity, loss aversion and variable rewards:** false deadlines, loss-recovery prompts and near-miss wheels can pressure users. These are excluded. No expiring purchased currency, hidden recurring billing, automatic purchase, loss-chasing offer or spending-based difficulty adjustment.

The FTC's Epic case demonstrates why unnoticed purchasing is not an acceptable success metric: confusing controls and insufficient affirmative consent led to a $245 million refund order for unwanted charges [R4]. The European CPC principles likewise emphasize clear real-money pricing, avoidance of forced unwanted virtual currency, withdrawal rights and protection of vulnerable consumers [R11]. This is a design boundary, not a legal opinion about approval in every market.

## 2. What successful ad integrations tell us

Google's Boxing Star case study reports 15% higher revenue, 8% higher retention and 38% higher engagement after rewarded ads were introduced; it describes tutorial gating, reward design and frequency testing [R8]. GungHo introduced rewarded ads gradually in Puzzle & Dragons, monitored retention and player feedback, and then expanded placements [R9]. Netmarble's case study reports three times the retention among users who opted into ads [R10]. That comparison is strongly exposed to self-selection: already-engaged users may be more likely to watch. It does not prove ads triple retention.

The transferable pattern is **opt-in value exchange plus measurement**, not copying reported uplift. These are vendor-selected success stories, not independent randomized guarantees. Their eCPMs, player populations, session structures and monetisation maturity do not estimate Mega XO revenue.

### Frequency: use both game count and elapsed play

A timer alone can interrupt a thought or a clock. A game-count rule alone can over-serve players finishing short games and under-serve long sessions. Google's interstitial guidance calls for natural transitions and warns against surprise, recurrent and late-loading ads [R6]. AdMob can also cap impressions per app/ad unit over minutes, hours or days, but its documentation notes propagation and serving delays [R7]. App-level guards are still needed.

**V3.5 initial automatic-ad policy:**

- No automatic ads in the first app session or first 24 hours on that installation.
- At least five meaningful bot games completed in total before first eligibility.
- At least three completed meaningful bot games AND eight active-play minutes since the last automatic ad.
- At least eight wall-clock minutes since any full-screen ad, including a rewarded ad.
- At most two automatic ads per app session and six per UTC day. Account-side permits provide a further daily/time cap.
- Only when leaving a completed normal bot-game result for Home. No advertisements during moves, tutorial, pass-and-play, a live online match, queue acceptance, direct challenges, tournament fixtures, lobbies or recovery screens.
- No ads after resignation, timeout or aborted games in this pilot.
- A not-ready/no-fill/error ad is skipped. Gameplay must not wait for ad loading. A late response must recheck current state and must not appear over a new match.
- Remove Ads owners never receive automatic ads. Optional rewarded ads are separately disclosed and never auto-play.

Automatic Casual-result placements are not enabled in the initial pilot: synchronous online transitions and invitation acceptance deserve their own test before expansion. These initial numbers are deliberately conservative hypotheses. A heavy-ad variant is not justified before retention data exists.

## 3. The always-on page, banner, wheel and multiplier ideas

### Permanent destination: yes. Endless ad page: no.

Create **Rewards & Style**, a permanently discoverable destination with a real cosmetic gallery, previews, owned/equipped items, balances, free earning information and optional reward choices. Do not auto-load an ad just because the page opens. It must not be an ad farm or an empty page containing only ad inventory. Google restricts ads on screens without publisher content, and Apple rejects apps designed predominantly to display ads [R12, R13].

A persistent banner is not part of the initial rollout. On a dense 81-cell mobile strategy board, it takes space from controls and creates accidental-click risk. A future menu-only adaptive banner experiment must reserve space, be clearly labelled, avoid controls, stop when not visible and honor Remove Ads. No banner appears over gameplay or beneath a finger target.

### Rewarded ads: yes, guaranteed and capped

Offer **5 Cosmetic Credits** for one completed, verified rewarded ad, with a shared limit of four rewarded opportunities per UTC day and at least two minutes between starts. Declining does not remove any already-earned reward or prevent ordinary play. Do not require ad clicks, installs, purchases or tracking permission to receive a reward [R5]. A no-fill/error must produce an honest unavailable state, not a fake video.

**Why not ordinary Coins?** Existing Coins convert to Crowns and can enter pooled paid matches/tournaments. Google's reward rules require benefits to remain non-transferable [R5]. Treating wager-capable currency as an ad reward would need explicit provider/legal review. V3.5 therefore adds a strictly account-bound cosmetic balance: not purchasable, not convertible, not transferable, not stakeable, not part of wealth/Elo leaderboards. It unlocks decorative frames only. This conservative separation also prevents rewarded ads from inflating the competitive economy.

Players also have an ad-free earning route: 2 Cosmetic Credits per meaningful, server-verified free Casual queue completion, capped at 10 per UTC day. Tournament, Ranked, private/direct, forfeited and short games do not count. Credits have no cash value or expiration.

### Timed multiplier: bounded cosmetic-only experiment

The alternative rewarded choice is a **2x Casual cosmetic-credit boost**. It doubles the ordinary 2-credit free Casual completion reward for games started within the following ten minutes, with at most 10 extra credits per UTC day. A qualifying game started before expiry can finish later: do not hurry a player into bad moves. It does not multiply Coins, Crowns, purchases, stakes, tournament payouts, Ranked rewards or Elo. No stacking or extension of an active boost; both rewarded choices share the four-ad quota. Match completion comes from server history, never a client-submitted winner or duration.

### Wheel: not in this release

Google does not categorically ban random rewarded benefits: the chance-based nature and all possible rewards must be disclosed in advance [R5]. Nevertheless a wheel introduces probability disclosure, trust, animation/accessibility and abuse complexity without a demonstrated benefit to this strategy game. Use a guaranteed reward first. A future cosmetic-only wheel would need published probabilities, no purchase for extra spins, no fabricated near misses, the same quota and a measured advantage over a fixed reward.

## 4. Product catalogue and merchandising

Retain the existing Crown packs (100 / 525 / 1,100) without increasing base-game scarcity. Add:

| Product | Authored price hypothesis, USD | Delivery |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Starter | 0.99 | 100 Crowns and the Origin decorative frame, once per account; no fake deadline |
| Remove Ads | 3.99 | Permanent removal of all automatic ads; optional rewarded ads remain explicitly opt-in |
| Style Collection | 2.99 | Orbit and Crown decorative frames; no competitive effect |

Prices are catalogue/planning hypotheses only. The device store supplies the actual localized price and purchase sheet. Do not display guessed exchange-rate prices. Do not advertise percentage savings until calculated from like-for-like localized products. Never offer an already-owned non-consumable as a new purchase. Restore is separate from buying; duplicate receipts cannot duplicate entitlements or currency.

The smallest Crown pack remains a clear choice rather than preselecting the largest. Show pack quantity and Coin equivalent alongside its real price. Explain that paying does not guarantee a match win or tournament profit. A purchase preview lists exactly what is delivered, whether it is one-time or repeatable, and whether optional rewarded ads remain. A second explicit confirmation opens the native store. No loss-triggered popups, automatic carts or countdown pressure.

No subscription yet: replay analysis/puzzles are deferred and there is not yet a defensible recurring service to sell. No battle pass until there is enough cosmetic content and an explicit policy for earnable progress without daily obligation.

## 5. Engineering and platform requirements

Ads and new purchases remain disabled without real provider configuration and trusted audience/territory eligibility. An unknown-age audience is not silently treated as adult. A neutral age/parental-consent policy and platform controls must be configured before deployment. Do not infer willingness to pay from financial vulnerability or target children for spending.

Google's SSV guide documents ECDSA verification, immutable raw query data, rotating public keys and unique transactions [R14]. Use server-issued, actor-bound reward tickets, expected ad units/reward values, expiry and idempotent settlement. The client may show verification pending, but cannot credit an account through a watched flag. Cash-like competitive balances require especially strict separation.

Apple requires tracking permission for cross-company tracking and forbids gating or rewarding that permission [R15]. ATT consent is not the same as the voluntary ad-view decision or a complete privacy/legal basis. Use the real CMP/UMP, required privacy options, accurate privacy disclosures and suitable ad ratings. Contextual ads are not automatically exempt from all privacy obligations. Avoid collecting precise location, ad IDs or unrelated personal data for our own event reporting.

India-specific release gate: MeitY's current official index includes the 2025 Act and April 2026 rules/enforcement notifications [R16]. The existing purchasable, convertible, pooled-stake economy needs current qualified review; lack of cash-out alone is not a legal clearance. This branch does not enable paid-entry operation or claim country/platform approval.

## 6. Experiment and business model

Start with a rewarded-only cohort and a no-ad control before expanding the hybrid pilot. Assign cohorts persistently, randomize at account level, stratify reporting by platform/territory, and compare all assigned users (intention-to-treat), not only viewers or payers. Do not change difficulty, opponents or reward eligibility between arms.

Primary decision: incremental net revenue per acquired user over a fixed observation window, including IAP, advertising, store fees, refunds and variable service costs. Also inspect D1/D7 retention, first-match completion, qualified games/user, ad-dismissal exit rate, crashes/latency, opt-in, fill, completion, purchase refunds, revenue concentration and no-ad entitlement failures. Ad revenue rising while IAP or retention falls can be a net loss.

Ad revenue/day = DAU * eligible-viewer fraction * impressions/viewer/day * realized eCPM / 1,000. Never multiply app opens by eCPM or assume 100% fill. Remove Ads pricing must exceed the expected discounted automatic-ad contribution forgone for that purchaser cohort, not the average of all players. Rewarded engagement may remain after ad removal but is not guaranteed.

A reporting/model script will expose low/base/high assumptions and calculate break-even rather than assert a revenue forecast. No eCPM or conversion assumption is represented as measured Mega XO data. Keep a no-ad control and predefine a retention non-inferiority margin before testing; a candidate 1 percentage-point D7 margin is a product hypothesis, not proof from a small sample. Run a power calculation using beta baselines and do not stop a test at the first favorable result.
